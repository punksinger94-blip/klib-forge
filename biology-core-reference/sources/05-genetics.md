# Classical and Molecular Genetics

Gregor Mendel established the principles of inheritance using pea plants.

## Key terms

- Gene: a unit of heredity; allele: an alternative version of a gene.
- Genotype: the genetic makeup; phenotype: the observable trait.
- Homozygous: two identical alleles; heterozygous: two different alleles.
- Dominant alleles mask recessive alleles in heterozygotes.

## Mendel's laws

- Law of segregation: the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each
  gamete carries one allele.
- Law of independent assortment: alleles of different genes assort independently during
  gamete formation (for genes on different chromosomes).

A monohybrid cross of two heterozygotes (Aa x Aa) gives a genotypic ratio of 1:2:1 and a
phenotypic ratio of 3:1 dominant to recessive. A test cross with a homozygous recessive
reveals an unknown genotype.

## Beyond simple dominance

- Incomplete dominance: heterozygote shows a blended/intermediate phenotype.
- Codominance: both alleles fully expressed (for example, AB blood type).
- Multiple alleles: more than two alleles in a population (ABO blood groups).
- Polygenic inheritance: many genes affect one trait (height, skin color).
- Sex-linked genes lie on sex chromosomes; X-linked recessive traits appear more often
  in males, who are hemizygous for X.
- Linked genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together; crossing over can
  separate them, with recombination frequency proportional to distance.
