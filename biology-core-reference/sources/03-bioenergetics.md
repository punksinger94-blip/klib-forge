# Bioenergetics and Cellular Respiration

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the cell's main energy currency; energy is released when
its terminal phosphate bond is hydrolyzed to ADP + Pi. Aerobic respiration oxidizes
glucose to carbon dioxide and water, capturing energy as ATP.

Overall: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 -> 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy (ATP + heat).

It proceeds in four stages.

## 1. Glycolysis (in the cytosol)

One molecule of glucose (6 carbons) is split into two molecules of pyruvate (3 carbons
each). It does not require oxygen. Per glucose, the NET yield is 2 ATP (4 made by
substrate-level phosphorylation minus 2 invested), 2 NADH, and 2 pyruvate.

## 2. Pyruvate oxidation (mitochondrial matrix)

Each pyruvate is converted to acetyl-CoA, releasing one CO2 and producing one NADH. Per
glucose (two pyruvate) this yields 2 NADH and 2 CO2.

## 3. Citric acid cycle / Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix)

Acetyl-CoA enters the cycle. Per single turn the cycle produces 3 NADH, 1 FADH2, 1 GTP
(or ATP) by substrate-level phosphorylation, and releases 2 CO2. Because each glucose
yields two acetyl-CoA, the cycle turns twice per glucose: 6 NADH, 2 FADH2, 2 GTP, and
4 CO2.

## 4. Oxidative phosphorylation (inner mitochondrial membrane)

The electron transport chain passes electrons from NADH and FADH2 through a series of
complexes, pumping protons (H+) into the intermembrane space to build a proton-motive
force. ATP synthase then uses the proton gradient to make ATP (chemiosmosis). Oxygen is
the final electron acceptor, forming water. Using modern consensus values (about 2.5 ATP
per NADH and 1.5 ATP per FADH2), complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose yields
roughly 30 to 32 ATP. Older textbooks cite 36 to 38 ATP.

## Anaerobic conditions

Without oxygen, fermentation regenerates NAD+ so glycolysis can continue. Lactic acid
fermentation (in muscle) produces lactate; alcoholic fermentation (in yeast) produces
ethanol and CO2. Fermentation yields only the 2 net ATP from glycolysis.
