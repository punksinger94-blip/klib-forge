# Cell Signaling and Neurophysiology

## Cell signaling

Cells communicate through signal transduction: a signaling molecule (ligand) binds a
receptor, triggering a response. Receptors include cell-surface receptors (G
protein-coupled receptors, receptor tyrosine kinases, ligand-gated ion channels) and
intracellular receptors for hydrophobic signals such as steroid hormones. Many pathways
use second messengers (such as cyclic AMP or calcium ions) and phosphorylation cascades
to amplify the signal.

## The neuron and the membrane potential

Neurons maintain a resting membrane potential, the inside being negative relative to the
outside, typically about -70 millivolts (mV). This is set by the unequal distribution of
ions (high K+ inside, high Na+ outside), the membrane's higher permeability to K+ at
rest, and the sodium-potassium pump (3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in per ATP).

## The action potential

A stimulus that depolarizes the membrane to threshold (about -55 mV) triggers an action
potential, an all-or-none event:

1. Depolarization: voltage-gated sodium channels open; Na+ rushes in; the inside becomes
   positive (peaks near +30 to +40 mV).
2. Repolarization: sodium channels inactivate and voltage-gated potassium channels open;
   K+ flows out, restoring negativity.
3. Hyperpolarization: brief overshoot below the resting potential before the pump and
   channels restore the resting state.

The signal propagates along the axon; in myelinated axons it jumps between nodes of
Ranvier (saltatory conduction). At the synapse, the action potential triggers release of
neurotransmitter, carrying the signal to the next cell.
