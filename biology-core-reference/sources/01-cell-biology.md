# Cell Biology and Organelles

The cell is the basic unit of life. Two fundamental cell types exist. Prokaryotic
cells (bacteria and archaea) lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound
organelles; their genetic material sits in a region called the nucleoid. Eukaryotic
cells (protists, fungi, plants, animals) enclose their DNA in a nucleus and contain
membrane-bound organelles.

## Major eukaryotic organelles and their functions

- Nucleus: stores DNA, controls gene expression; bounded by a double-membrane nuclear
  envelope with nuclear pores. The nucleolus inside it assembles ribosomes.
- Ribosomes: sites of protein synthesis (translation). Made of rRNA and protein; not
  membrane-bound. Free ribosomes make cytosolic proteins; bound ribosomes on the rough
  ER make secretory and membrane proteins.
- Rough endoplasmic reticulum (rough ER): studded with ribosomes; folds and modifies
  proteins. Smooth ER: synthesizes lipids, metabolizes carbohydrates, detoxifies drugs,
  and stores calcium.
- Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids into vesicles for
  secretion or delivery. It has a cis (receiving) face and a trans (shipping) face.
- Mitochondria: the site of aerobic cellular respiration; generate most of the cell's
  ATP. They have a double membrane; the inner membrane folds into cristae and encloses
  the matrix. Mitochondria contain their own DNA.
- Lysosomes: contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion and recycling.
- Peroxisomes: break down fatty acids and detoxify hydrogen peroxide.
- Chloroplasts (plants, algae): site of photosynthesis; contain chlorophyll in stacked
  thylakoids and have their own DNA.
- Cytoskeleton: microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments, and microtubules
  provide shape, support, and tracks for transport and motility.

## Membranes

All cell membranes follow the fluid mosaic model: a phospholipid bilayer (hydrophilic
heads outward, hydrophobic tails inward) with embedded and peripheral proteins,
cholesterol, and carbohydrates. The membrane is selectively permeable.

## Endosymbiotic theory

Mitochondria and chloroplasts are thought to have originated from free-living
prokaryotes engulfed by an ancestral host cell, evidenced by their double membranes,
circular DNA, and own ribosomes.
