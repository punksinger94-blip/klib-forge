# Enzymes and Enzyme Kinetics

Enzymes are biological catalysts (mostly proteins) that speed reactions by lowering the
activation energy. They are not consumed and do not change the reaction equilibrium.

## Mechanism

The substrate binds the enzyme's active site. The induced-fit model holds that the
active site changes shape slightly to fit the substrate, forming an enzyme-substrate
complex. Enzymes are specific and are sensitive to temperature and pH, each having an
optimum. Cofactors (inorganic ions) and coenzymes (organic molecules such as vitamins)
assist catalysis.

## Michaelis-Menten kinetics

For many enzymes the initial reaction velocity relates to substrate concentration by:

v0 = (Vmax * [S]) / (Km + [S])

- Vmax is the maximum velocity, approached when the enzyme is saturated with substrate.
- Km, the Michaelis constant, equals the substrate concentration at which the reaction
  rate is half of Vmax. A low Km indicates high affinity of the enzyme for its
  substrate; a high Km indicates low affinity.

## Inhibition

- Competitive inhibitors resemble the substrate and bind the active site; they raise the
  apparent Km but leave Vmax unchanged, and their effect can be overcome by adding more
  substrate.
- Noncompetitive (allosteric) inhibitors bind a site other than the active site, change
  the enzyme's shape, and lower Vmax; Km is unchanged and adding substrate does not
  relieve the inhibition.
- Feedback inhibition: the end product of a pathway inhibits an earlier enzyme to
  regulate flux.
