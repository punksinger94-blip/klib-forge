# The Cell Cycle, Mitosis, and Meiosis

## The cell cycle

The eukaryotic cell cycle is interphase followed by the mitotic (M) phase. Interphase
has three parts: G1 (growth), S (DNA synthesis/replication), and G2 (growth and
preparation). Checkpoints (notably G1/S and G2/M) ensure the cell is ready to proceed;
DNA damage or incomplete replication halts the cycle.

## Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells and proceeds in four
phases, in order: prophase (chromatin condenses, spindle forms), metaphase (chromosomes
align at the metaphase plate), anaphase (sister chromatids separate to opposite poles),
and telophase (nuclear envelopes re-form). Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm, producing
two cells.

## Meiosis

Meiosis produces gametes and consists of two divisions, meiosis I and meiosis II,
following a single round of DNA replication. The result is four genetically distinct
haploid cells (n), each with half the chromosome number of the diploid (2n) parent.

Genetic variation arises from crossing over (exchange of segments between homologous
chromosomes during prophase I) and independent assortment of homologous pairs at
metaphase I. In meiosis I homologous chromosomes separate (reducing ploidy from 2n to
n); in meiosis II sister chromatids separate, as in mitosis.

## Mitosis versus meiosis

Mitosis: one division, two diploid identical cells, for growth and repair. Meiosis: two
divisions, four haploid varied cells, for sexual reproduction.
