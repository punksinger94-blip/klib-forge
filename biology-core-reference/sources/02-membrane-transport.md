# Membrane Transport

Movement across the selectively permeable plasma membrane is either passive (no cellular
energy) or active (requires energy, usually ATP).

## Passive transport

- Simple diffusion: net movement of molecules from high to low concentration (down the
  concentration gradient) until equilibrium. Small nonpolar molecules (O2, CO2) cross
  directly.
- Facilitated diffusion: passive movement of polar molecules or ions through channel or
  carrier proteins, still down the gradient.
- Osmosis: diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of
  lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration. Tonicity describes the
  effect of a solution on cell water: in a hypotonic solution cells gain water and may
  lyse; in a hypertonic solution cells lose water and shrink (crenate/plasmolyze); an
  isotonic solution causes no net movement.

## Active transport

Active transport moves solutes against their concentration gradient and requires energy.

- Primary active transport uses ATP directly. The sodium-potassium pump
  (Na+/K+-ATPase) is the classic example: for each ATP hydrolyzed it pumps 3 sodium
  ions (Na+) out of the cell and 2 potassium ions (K+) into the cell. This maintains
  the electrochemical gradient and contributes to the negative resting membrane
  potential.
- Secondary active transport (cotransport) uses the gradient established by primary
  transport. Symporters move two solutes the same direction; antiporters move them in
  opposite directions.

## Bulk transport

Large materials move by vesicles: endocytosis (phagocytosis for solids, pinocytosis for
fluids, receptor-mediated endocytosis for specific molecules) brings material in;
exocytosis releases material out.
