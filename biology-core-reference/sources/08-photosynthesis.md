# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It occurs
in chloroplasts. Overall:

6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2.

It has two stages.

## Light-dependent reactions (in the thylakoid membranes)

Chlorophyll in photosystem II (P680) and photosystem I (P700) absorbs light. Water is
split (photolysis), releasing oxygen (O2) as a by-product, electrons, and protons.
Electrons pass down an electron transport chain, pumping protons into the thylakoid
lumen; ATP synthase makes ATP (photophosphorylation), and photosystem I reduces NADP+ to
NADPH. The products ATP and NADPH power the next stage.

## Light-independent reactions / Calvin cycle (in the stroma)

The Calvin cycle fixes carbon. The enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO2 to the five-carbon sugar
RuBP (carbon fixation). Using ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, the cycle reduces
the fixed carbon to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), a three-carbon sugar; two G3P can
form glucose, and RuBP is regenerated. The Calvin cycle does not directly require light
but depends on the ATP and NADPH the light reactions supply.

## Variations

C3 plants fix carbon directly via RuBisCO. C4 and CAM plants concentrate CO2 to reduce
photorespiration in hot or dry climates; CAM plants open stomata at night.
